### Island Fundamentals and Getting to Nagishima
Nagishima is a scenic island located 18 km south of Shiomi Port on the mainland. Covering an area of 8.6 km², it is home to 1,240 residents (as of March 2026). The name Nagishima originates from Yuunagi, the calm evening period when the wind completely stops.

**Ferry Transportation: The Nagi-maru**
Access to the island is provided by the ferry Nagi-maru, which completes the crossing between Shiomi Port and Nagishima in 50 minutes.
- Outbound Schedule (Shiomi Port to Nagishima): 4 departures daily at 7:10, 10:30, 14:00, and 17:20.
- Return Schedule (Nagishima to Shiomi Port): 4 departures daily at 8:30, 12:00, 15:40, and 18:50 (last ferry).
- Weather Limitations: Ferry service is completely canceled if wave heights reach 3 meters or higher.

**Important Fare Updates**
Travelers relying solely on the 2026 Tourist Guide might be caught off guard. While the guide lists standard one-way fares as 2,400 JPY for adults and 1,200 JPY for children, a local resident blog post from May 2026 highlights an April 1 price increase driven by rising fuel costs:
- Adult One-Way Fare: Updated to 2,800 JPY (up from 2,400 JPY).
- Child One-Way Fare (Elementary): Updated to 1,400 JPY (up from 1,200 JPY).
- Bicycle Surcharge: Remains unchanged at 600 JPY.
- Traveler Caution: Outdated promotional pamphlets listing old rates are still placed around the port window!

### Exploring Nagishima – Sightseeing, Local Transport, and Accommodations
Once on the island, visitors have several historic sights, local craft workshops, and cozy inns to explore.

**Sightseeing Highlights**
- Nagishima Lighthouse: Built in 1898 (Meiji 31), this historic white lighthouse features 96 steps to the top. Admission is 300 JPY (open 9:00–16:30). While the official guide lists its height as 18 m, a recent typhoon prompt led to installing a new antenna, bringing its total tip height to 21 m (though the structure itself remains 18 m). Crucially, local innkeeper Ms. Hamada notes that the lighthouse is closed on Mondays—a detail missing from the main travel guide!
- Shiomi-ga-hama Beach: Located on the western shore, famous for sea waters that turn mirror-still during evening calm.
- Salt Workshop 'Amane': Offers 20-minute reservation-only tours showcasing how local seawater is boiled down to produce Nagi Salt and famous salt lemons.

**Getting Around the Island**
- Island Loop: The main circular road stretching around the island is 11 km long. Bicycle rentals are available near the port for 1,000 JPY per day.
- Local Bus Service: The 2026 Tourist Guide lists 6 bus trips per day connecting the port and lighthouse. However, due to a driver resignation in April, the service was reduced to 5 trips per day.

**Local Hospitality & Annual Events**
- Inn Matsuri (Minshuku Matsuri): Located a 5-minute walk from the port, this intimate 6-room inn operated by Ms. Hamada costs 9,800 JPY per adult and 6,500 JPY for elementary children (includes 1 night and 2 meals, featuring horse mackerel dried fish grilled with island salt for breakfast).
- Nagi Festival: Held annually on the first Saturday of August. Features vibrant food stalls at the port square and a 8:30 PM fireworks display, which can be viewed directly from the second-floor windows of Inn Matsuri.

### Analyzing Tourism Patterns and Trends
Understanding visitor flow is essential for planning a peaceful trip to Nagishima. Comparative tourist statistics from 2024 and 2025 reveal strong growth and sharp seasonality.

**Key Tourist Data Findings (2024 vs. 2025)**
- Consistent Growth: Tourism grew across every single month from 2024 to 2025.
- Peak Season (August): August is by far the busiest month, jumping from 5,870 visitors in 2024 to 6,320 visitors in 2025, driven heavily by the annual Nagi Festival.
- Secondary Peaks: July (3,540 in 2025) and May (2,950 in 2025) represent major high-volume months, corresponding with warm weather and spring holidays.
- Low Season (Winter): December is the quietest month (690 visitors in 2024; 720 in 2025), with January and February also remaining below 900 total visitors.

By examining these numbers, travelers seeking lower crowds can target shoulder months like April (2,110 visitors) or October (2,190 visitors) when weather remains pleasant but crowds are less than half of peak August volume.
